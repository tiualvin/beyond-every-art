    From the studio

    Apps

    Beyond Every Art is a magazine about colour, materials, and how things are made. These are the apps we want to make to go alongside it — for the moment you finish a piece about a limited palette and want to go and mix one yourself.

    None of them exist yet. Nothing here can be downloaded and nothing has a date. If you would like to hear when one opens, there is a form at the bottom of this page — and which ones you tick is honestly how we will decide what to make first.

    01

    Beyond Every Art

    Art, colour, materials, exhibitions, and creative practice.

    The magazine in your pocket, made for reading rather than scrolling. Save a piece for the train and it is there without a signal. Underline a paragraph, keep a note next to it, and find both again a year later.

    It opens on one thing instead of a feed: a piece worth your morning, a pigment to look at, a small exercise you could finish before the coffee goes cold. And when you want to wander, you can go by artist or by material rather than by date — everything we have written about lapis, or about egg tempera, gathered in one place.

    The one we are making first. If you only ever use one of these, it should probably be this one.

    02

    Dapple

    A quiet place to colour.

    Colouring for grown-ups, on drawings made by artists rather than generated by a machine. The paint behaves like paint: watercolour blooms out into damp paper, pencil catches on the grain, marker pools where you rest your hand.

    Nothing hurries you. There is no streak to keep and no timer running. Stay inside the lines if you like — the app will help you — or do not, and let the colour run over the edge. A new page each day is free, and anything you finish comes out big enough to frame.

    Watercolour, pencil, pastel, marker, ink. Palettes you mix yourself, palettes chosen by the artist who drew the page, and paper that behaves like paper under the brush.

    03

    Morrow

    Make a little space for yourself.

    Part planner, part sketchbook, part diary — somewhere to lay out a week, write by hand, stick things in, and think. A notebook rather than a productivity system: nothing scores you, and nothing turns red when you skip a day.

    Its one real idea is the mark. Instead of picking a face for how the day went, you leave a colour, a brushstroke, a shape, a small drawing, a photograph. Thirty of them make a month you can read at a glance, and a year of them makes something closer to a painting — which is the picture on this page.

    Yours, and nobody else's. What you write and draw stays on your own device unless you ask it to sync, it travels encrypted when it does, and you can take all of it out, or delete it, at any point.

    04

    Echo Garden

    Paint a world that sings back.

    A game, more or less. You arrive somewhere quiet and half-finished — an overgrown garden, an observatory adrift, a temple under water — and find the thing that has lost its colour and its voice. Then you draw, and it answers back. Dots ring like small bells, long strokes pull at strings, a spiral turns into a voice, a wash of colour opens out into something like weather.

    You do not need to be able to draw. The game tunes whatever you make, so it comes out sounding right either way. It is also the furthest off of the four, and the one most likely to change shape before you see it.
